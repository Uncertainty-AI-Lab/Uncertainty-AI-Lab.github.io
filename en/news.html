A Solution for LLM Hallucination! Uncertainty Characterization Based on Evidence Theory

Huan Ma, Jingdong Chen, et al.

Large Language Models (LLMs) often face "hallucination" problems, outputting unreliable information. Uncertainty estimation, as a key indicator for assessing the reliability of model outputs, is crucial for enhancing LLM trustworthiness and supporting downstream tasks. However, traditional probability-based uncertainty estimation methods struggle to effectively capture the uncertainty of generated responses and perform poorly in LLM scenarios. This paper reveals the core reason for their failure: probability methods lose evidence intensity information during normalization. Based on this, we propose the LogTokU (Logits-induced Token Uncertainty) framework, which models uncertainty as aleatoric uncertainty (AU) and epistemic uncertainty (EU) by utilizing the evidence intensity of LLM-generated next tokens, enabling real-time estimation of large model uncertainty. It significantly outperforms traditional methods in dynamic decoding and QA reliability estimation, enhancing LLM reliability, expanding the application scope of large model uncertainty, while providing new insights for exploring downstream tasks in multiple fields. It has attracted attention from researchers at institutions such as MIT and Michigan State University.

CVPR2025｜Can our brain signals faithfully reflect what we see?

Haitao Wu, Qing Li, et al.

Despite humans' powerful perception and cognitive abilities, constrained by attention resources, visual memory capacity, and other factors, when external visual stimuli are converted into brain signals, some information is inevitably lost, especially high-frequency details. We refer to this information gap caused by systematic processing as the System GAP. Meanwhile, the dynamics of cognition and perception, as well as technical acquisition noise, further widen the gap between signals and original visual stimuli, which we call the Random GAP. These factors collectively lead to information inconsistency and modal differences between brain signals and visual stimuli, increasing the difficulty of aligning images with brain signals and affecting model generalization performance. In this study, we propose the Uncertainty-aware Blur Prior (UBP) method to mitigate information loss caused by System GAP and Random GAP in visual neural decoding. Experimental results show that UBP significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on multiple datasets. On the THING-EEG dataset, UBP achieved a 13.7% improvement in Top-1 accuracy and a 9.8% improvement in Top-5 accuracy. This method provides new research perspectives for the brain-computer interface field by introducing uncertainty awareness and biological priors into visual neural decoding. Simultaneously, UBP offers important insights for other multimodal tasks, especially when facing tasks with modal gaps and information imbalances.
